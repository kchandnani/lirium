Google wants the Gemini app to stop answering questions and start doing chores. In a Nov. 17 blog post, Google said Tasks in Gemini is "rolling out free of charge for hundreds of millions of people across the U.S., Japan, Korea, Canada, Australia, and India, using our new Gemini 4 Hydrogen model," with "a set of advanced features, starting in the U.S." ([Google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/tasks-in-gemini-free-new-features/)). The headline on that post is "Gemini can now make the calls, pay the bills, and finish your tasks."

That's a big promise for a free tool. Our pitch: don't roll it out. Give it one chore you already do every week, run it for a week, and write down every time Gemini stops to ask before it acts. That record is the whole test.

## What "free" covers, and who it doesn't

Start with the account question, because it decides whether you can run the test at all. Google's help page lists three requirements: "Be 18 or over," "Sign in to the Gemini app with a personal Google Account," and "Have History on." Then the line that matters for most small businesses, as the page reads today: "For now, this feature isn't available if you sign in with a work or school Google Account" ([Gemini Apps Help](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/17094507?hl=en)).

So if your team lives in Google Workspace, Tasks won't show up on your company login. The Nov. 17 release notes repeat the personal account rule for three of the four new U.S. features: Google Pay checkout, texting and email forwarding are each listed for "users in the U.S. (18+ with personal Google accounts)" ([Gemini release notes](https://gemini.google/release-notes/)). Running business chores from a personal account means vendor emails, calls and receipts land there, not in the company's account. Decide if that's fine before you forward a single invoice.

"Free" also means something narrow. The help page says Tasks are "available without a Google AI subscription" in those six countries and "only available in the Gemini mobile app, the Gemini app on Mac, and the Gemini web app" ([Gemini Apps Help](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/17094507?hl=en)). It isn't a free API tier, and it doesn't make Hydrogen free to build on. If you're wiring Hydrogen into your own tools, our [Nov. 24 post](/blog/gemini-4-hydrogen-vs-carbon-price) covers what the API charges.

## What it can actually do in the U.S.

Google's blog says you "flip the home-screen toggle from Chat to Task" and tell Gemini what you need ([Google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/tasks-in-gemini-free-new-features/)). The Nov. 17 release notes list four U.S. additions ([Gemini release notes](https://gemini.google/release-notes/)):

- **Calls to businesses.** Gemini can place calls "even dialing multiple shops at once, scheduling calls during business hours, or placing recurring calls." Google's footnote, as of Nov. 17: calling "is not currently available in Indiana, Louisiana, Minnesota, Montana, and Nebraska" ([Google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/tasks-in-gemini-free-new-features/)).
- **Email.** Forward a thread to gemini@google.com from "Gmail, Apple Mail, or Outlook," and Google says it "always replies privately 1:1 to you."
- **Texts.** After "a one-time phone verification," you can text Gemini from Google Messages or iMessage. You need "a verified U.S. phone number and an eligible carrier."
- **Google Pay checkout.** Google says it is rolling out "in English on web, Android, and iOS" for U.S. users "18+ with personal Google accounts."

## Where it stops and asks, and where it doesn't

This is what you're testing. The rules differ by action.

**Buying.** The release notes are the clearest here:

> Nothing is purchased without your go-ahead: once you give your explicit 1-tap and device approval, Gemini finishes checkout with Google Pay so your actual card number stays separated from the merchant but you still earn your card's points, cashback, or purchase protections during checkout.

That's Google's claim, from the [Gemini release notes](https://gemini.google/release-notes/). You "set your item and maximum price limit when starting a Task," and Gemini "stages the checkout and sends you an itemized summary to review." Google doesn't set the cap. You do. One caveat: Google's blog says Gemini "is designed to ask for your confirmation or pre-approval" before it clicks Buy. "Pre-approval" leaves room for checkouts you approved up front, so don't assume a tap on every order.

**Sending and booking.** The texting entry says Gemini "always pauses for your 1-tap confirmation before taking major actions like sending or booking" ([Gemini release notes](https://gemini.google/release-notes/)). The help page says Gemini "is designed to always ask for your review and confirmation" before actions such as "sending communications, deleting your data, sharing sensitive info, making purchases, completing financial transactions, and filling out and submitting web forms" ([Gemini Apps Help](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/17094507?hl=en)).

**Calling.** The release notes say "You can review, approve the call plan before Gemini dials" ([Gemini release notes](https://gemini.google/release-notes/)). That's approval of a plan, not a tap before each call. Once you approve a plan with recurring calls, we found nothing in Google's help page, blog or release notes saying Gemini checks back before each one. Treat the call plan as the only gate and read it closely.

Two more caveats. Google's blog says routine actions can be set to "Always allow" so it "doesn't pester you" ([Google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/tasks-in-gemini-free-new-features/)). For the test week, don't. And the help page warns that "if a schedule runs when you are offline, you may not be able to stop Gemini from completing an unintended action" ([Gemini Apps Help](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/17094507?hl=en)). Run the test only while you're around to answer.

## The guardrails we'd set first

**Pick your own cap, low.** Google says you "can set a hard spending cap upfront, and Gemini is designed to stay within your budget" ([Google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/tasks-in-gemini-free-new-features/)). "Designed to" is Google's wording, not a guarantee. Set the cap to the most you'd be fine losing on one bad order.

**Never paste a card number into the chat.** Google's own instructions: "Do not enter sign-in info, payment details, or info you may find sensitive directly into the task thread." If a site needs it, Google says to take control of the browser and enter it on the page ([Gemini Apps Help](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/17094507?hl=en)).

**Know what the other end hears.** Google says Gemini "identifies itself as an AI assistant, won't share payment or sensitive account details over the phone, and allows businesses to opt out at any time" ([Gemini release notes](https://gemini.google/release-notes/)). The opt-out link in Google's blog goes to a Business Profile help page, where businesses can say "Please stop calling my business" or switch off automated calls in their profile settings ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7690269?hl=en)). That page describes Google's automated calls generally and doesn't mention Gemini by name. It does say "these calls are monitored and recorded for quality assurance." If you call vendors you know, give them a heads up.

## A worked example: a toner restock

**This is an example, not a test we ran.** Say a six-person insurance office in Ohio runs out of printer toner every few weeks, and the office manager wants Gemini to handle it. The cap below is our example, not a Google number.

1. **Set up.** She signs into the Gemini app with her personal Google account (the office's Workspace login won't show Tasks), switches from Chat to Task and types: "Call the two nearest office supply stores and ask if they have [toner model] in stock today. If neither does, find it online and check out with Google Pay. Cap: $150."
2. **Approval point one: the call plan.** Gemini shows its call plan (in our example, the two stores and the question). She checks that it's only those two stores and that the script doesn't promise anything. She approves. Ohio isn't on Google's [no-calling list](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/tasks-in-gemini-free-new-features/).
3. **Gemini calls.** Google says Gemini identifies itself as an AI, so both stores should hear that. She gets a summary and a transcript.
4. **Approval point two: checkout.** Neither store has it. Gemini finds it online under the cap and sends an itemized summary. She checks the model number, quantity and shipping, then taps approve on her phone.
5. **The log.** One line per event: what Gemini wanted to do, whether it asked first, and whether the result was right.

At the end of the week, the log answers one question: did Gemini ever act where she expected a pause? If yes, that chore goes back to a person. If no, try a second chore next week. This is the same rule we used when [Meta's Muse started sending QuickBooks invoices](/blog/meta-muse-quickbooks-invoices): earn trust one action at a time.
