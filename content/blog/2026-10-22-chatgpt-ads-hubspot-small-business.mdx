Most ad channels catch people while they're scrolling. ChatGPT catches them while they're working through a decision. That is the whole pitch, and since May a small business can buy into it without an agency. OpenAI says ChatGPT Ads passed $1 billion in annualized revenue run rate in under 200 days, and that the introduction of Ads Manager in May "opened the platform to small and medium-sized businesses, which now represent a material share of the business" ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/expanding-access-to-ai-with-chatgpt-ads/)).

Then on September 16, HubSpot said: "As the first CRM to integrate with ChatGPT Ads, you can now reach buyers directly in ChatGPT" ([HubSpot](https://www.hubspot.com/company-news/fall-26-spotlight)). "First" is HubSpot's claim. The useful part is what comes after the click.

The demand problem is real. In Small Business Majority's Q3 Voice of Main Street survey, published September 16, 85% of owners said attracting new customers is difficult, and 72% said the same about finding the right marketing channels ([Small Business Majority](https://smallbusinessmajority.org/sites/default/files/research-reports/2026-Q3-Voice-of-Main-Street-Report.pdf), 222 owners, margin of error ±6%). Our take: ChatGPT ads are worth a capped, tracked test for businesses that sell something people research first. They are not worth it if leads will sit in an inbox.

## How Ads Manager works for a small advertiser

You sign in at ads.openai.com with your ChatGPT login and can paste your website URL so Ads Manager drafts your first ad ([OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001213-ads-manager-account-setup)). Country, currency and time zone can't be changed after the account is created. Ads go through review before they run. Self-serve is available in the United States and dozens of other countries ([OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001245-ads-manager-availability)).

The ad itself is small: your name, logo, a headline, a line of copy, an image and a landing page, shown below ChatGPT's answer ([OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001207-ads-in-chatgpt-the-basics)).

Targeting is not keywords. At the ad group level you write "context hints" describing the conversations where your service is relevant, and OpenAI says they "are not exact-match keywords and do not guarantee delivery" ([OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001207-ads-in-chatgpt-the-basics)). You can also target countries, platforms, custom audiences and, for most campaigns, states, cities, markets or postal codes ([OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001210-create-campaigns-for-chatgpt-ads)).

One audience caveat: OpenAI does not show ads to users on Plus, Pro or any Business plan ([OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001207-ads-in-chatgpt-the-basics)). If your buyers are companies that pay for ChatGPT Business, many of them will never see you.

## What it costs, and what isn't public

You pick one of three objectives: Views (billed per 1,000 impressions), Clicks (billed per valid click), or Conversions, which still bills by click or impression, never per conversion ([OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001210-create-campaigns-for-chatgpt-ads)). The minimum daily campaign budget in US dollars is $25 ([OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001413-daily-budgets)). For click campaigns, OpenAI recommends starting with a maximum bid of $3 to $5 per click, in a "relevance-weighted, second-price auction" ([OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001207-ads-in-chatgpt-the-basics)). That is a suggested starting bid cap, not an average price.

The daily budget is an average, not a ceiling. A campaign can spend up to twice the daily amount on a single day, capped at seven times the daily amount per Sunday to Saturday week ([OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001413-daily-budgets)). Billing is postpay: your card is charged whenever unpaid spend hits a threshold that starts low (OpenAI's example is $25) and rises with payment history, and again at month end ([OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001216-billing-payment)). Self-serve card accounts can't set an account-wide daily spending limit, so your campaign budget is your real control. Spend reporting can lag 7 to 8 hours, and paused ads can keep serving for up to 24 hours ([OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001216-billing-payment)).

What isn't public: average CPC, average CPM, or any benchmark. OpenAI says plainly that ChatGPT Ads "does not yet have performance benchmarks across advertisers, industries, or campaign types" ([OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001220-frequently-asked-questions)). Anyone quoting you a typical cost per click is quoting their own campaigns.

## One real test, with real numbers

IndoorMedia, an advertising company, published its own small test. It ran three campaigns through StackAdapt, one of OpenAI's technology partners, not through Ads Manager directly ([IndoorMedia](https://www.indoormedia.com/blog/we-spent-608-testing-chatgpt-ads-heres-what-happened/)). Total spend was about $608 for 139 clicks.

Its own two campaigns spent $377.89 and produced 8 leads and one $7,585 sale. Its click campaign paid $3.36 a click. Its impression campaign worked out to $15.10 a click. The third campaign, for Five Stars Auto Glass, paid $3.19 a click for 72 clicks and produced no confirmed business. Most of its customers phone in, and without a call-tracking number nobody could tell whether the ads drove calls ([IndoorMedia](https://www.indoormedia.com/blog/we-spent-608-testing-chatgpt-ads-heres-what-happened/)).

IndoorMedia called its result "a signal worth investigating rather than proof." We agree. The lesson isn't the one sale. It's that the channel produced a sale for the business that tracked its leads and a shrug for the one that couldn't.

For a larger data point, OpenAI says Angi cut its cost per lead 34% after moving to conversion-optimized campaigns ([OpenAI](https://ads.openai.com/customers/angi)). That's a national marketplace in a story OpenAI chose to publish, not a forecast for your shop.

## Wiring the leads into HubSpot

HubSpot's pitch: send ChatGPT traffic to a HubSpot landing page, capture leads, and "every contact gets enrolled in workflows and attributed alongside every other channel you run" ([HubSpot](https://www.hubspot.com/company-news/fall-26-spotlight)).

**Connect the account.** In HubSpot, go to Settings, then Marketing, then Ads, click Connect account and pick ChatGPT Ads. Turn on Auto tracking, which appends UTM parameters so HubSpot can tie contacts and deals to ChatGPT spend. The connection is listed for all HubSpot plans and needs Ads publishing permission. During the beta, HubSpot says only a ChatGPT "Tenant Admin" can approve it ([HubSpot Knowledge Base](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/connect-and-manage-chatgpt-ads-account-in-hubspot)). OpenAI calls the role "global admin" for ChatGPT Business and Enterprise organizations, and notes an Ads or HubSpot admin isn't automatically one ([OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001522-setting-up-chatgpt-ads-in-hubspot)). Find out who holds it before you start.

**Automate the first response.** Build one form just for this campaign and add a simple workflow in the form editor. On HubSpot's free tools that's one action, "Send an email after form submission." Marketing Hub Starter allows up to 10 actions per simple workflow, including an internal email notification and creating a task ([HubSpot Knowledge Base](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/forms/form-automations)). Full workflows need a Professional or Enterprise plan ([HubSpot Knowledge Base](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/workflows/create-workflows)). Starter covers most small shops: a confirmation email to the lead, an alert to whoever answers, a task due today. If you later want an AI agent qualifying these leads, see [what running one costs now](/blog/ai-agent-costs-90-percent-cheaper).

**Send outcomes back.** HubSpot ad conversion events can sync form submissions or lifecycle stage changes to ChatGPT so it optimizes toward real leads, not just clicks. You pick a consent property so only contacts who agreed to share their data are sent, and Starter accounts get up to 5 events ([HubSpot Knowledge Base](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/create-and-sync-ad-conversion-events-with-chatgpt)).

> A lead from ChatGPT arrives mid-decision. If nobody answers until tomorrow, you paid to start a conversation your competitor gets to finish.

## The test we'd run

Illustrative numbers; the budget is our choice, not OpenAI's.

1. **Budget:** a Clicks campaign at $30 a day for six Sunday to Saturday weeks. The weekly cap is $210, so the most you can spend is $1,260. Set the max bid at $3, the bottom of OpenAI's suggested range.
2. **Scope:** one service, one metro area, three to five context hints written as the questions customers really ask, one landing page, one HubSpot form.
3. **Before launch:** write down your break-even cost per lead. Average job value times gross margin times close rate. A $2,000 job at 40% margin and a 25% close rate breaks even at $200 a lead.
4. **Measure weekly:** spend, clicks, form fills, hours to first human reply, booked appointments, closed revenue. Add a call-tracking number if your customers phone.
5. **Decide at week six:** keep going if cost per lead is under break-even and at least one lead closed. Otherwise stop. Once conversion events are flowing, the Conversions objective is the next test, subject to OpenAI's eligibility rules.

## When not to bother

Skip it if nobody can reply to a new lead the same business day. Skip it if $750 a month, which is the $25 minimum over 30 days, would crowd out a channel that already makes you money. Skip it if customers mostly call and you won't set up call tracking. And skip it if you sell a walk-in or impulse purchase nobody researches in a chat first.
