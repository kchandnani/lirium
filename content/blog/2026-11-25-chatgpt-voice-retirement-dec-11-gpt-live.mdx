If you still talk to ChatGPT on Advanced or Standard voice, you have until Dec. 11 to pick when you switch. After that, OpenAI picks for you. OpenAI says it is "retiring Advanced and Standard voice from ChatGPT Voice on December 11, 2026" ([OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001638-retiring-advanced-and-standard-voice-in-chatgpt)). Dec. 11 is a Friday, 16 days after this post's Nov. 25 publish date.

The people this hits aren't the ones who tried voice once. It's the owner who talks through the day's jobs in the truck, the office manager who rehearses a tough customer call before dialing, the tech who dictates notes between stops. If that's a real part of your week, the switch is worth an afternoon of testing now instead of a surprise on a Friday in December.

## What OpenAI actually said

We read the Help Center article and the matching release note. Here's the timeline, in OpenAI's words.

- **Nov. 10, 2026:** "Users are notified about the retirement" ([OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001638-retiring-advanced-and-standard-voice-in-chatgpt)). The [ChatGPT release notes](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/6825453-chatgpt-release-notes) carry the same announcement under Nov. 10, 2026.
- **Until Dec. 11:** "Users can continue using Advanced or Standard voice" ([OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001638-retiring-advanced-and-standard-voice-in-chatgpt)).
- **Dec. 11, 2026:** "Users will no longer be able to select Advanced or Standard voice from the model picker in Settings > Voice. Users who previously selected these models will be defaulted to Live voice" ([OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001638-retiring-advanced-and-standard-voice-in-chatgpt)).

Live voice runs on GPT-Live. The release notes date its launch, as GPT-Live-1, to July 8, 2026 ([ChatGPT release notes](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/6825453-chatgpt-release-notes)). OpenAI says that since July, "voice usage has nearly doubled, and virtually all people using voice in ChatGPT now use GPT-Live" ([OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001638-retiring-advanced-and-standard-voice-in-chatgpt)). That's OpenAI's claim about its own product, and it doesn't come with a number we can check. And "most people moved" isn't the same as "it works for your job."

OpenAI also says it will "continue to improve areas like the depth and detail of responses, maintaining continuity across conversations, and better personalization" ([OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001638-retiring-advanced-and-standard-voice-in-chatgpt)). Read that as a list of things to check, because those are the exact places a voice workflow breaks.

Three things don't change on Dec. 11, per OpenAI:

- **Live video and screen-sharing** "will continue to be powered by Advanced voice until GPT-Live has this capability" ([OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001638-retiring-advanced-and-standard-voice-in-chatgpt)).
- **Old conversations** stay in your history "and can be continued in text or voice" ([OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001638-retiring-advanced-and-standard-voice-in-chatgpt)).
- **Read Aloud** isn't affected. OpenAI says the retirement "applies to Advanced and Standard voice conversations" and you can still use Read Aloud on written answers ([OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001638-retiring-advanced-and-standard-voice-in-chatgpt)).

## The custom GPT wrinkle

This is the part to read slowly if your staff talk to a custom GPT. Here's the exact line from the FAQ:

> Voice with custom GPTs will continue using Advanced voice until their deprecation on December 11, 2026.

That's from the [OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001638-retiring-advanced-and-standard-voice-in-chatgpt). On its own, "their" could be read two ways. The sentence links to OpenAI's custom GPT retirement FAQ, which settles it: "Custom GPTs are scheduled to retire on Dec 11, 2026," and on that date custom GPTs "and their GPT pages become inaccessible" ([OpenAI custom GPT FAQ](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001519-custom-gpt-retirement-and-migration-faq)).

So for most accounts, Dec. 11 is a double deadline. The voice your staff hear goes away, and so does the GPT they're talking to. There's one exception. OpenAI gives FedRAMP workspaces and Enterprise workspaces "with a qualifying, approved deferral" a Feb. 28, 2027 date, and says "this later date does not apply to every workspace" ([OpenAI custom GPT FAQ](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001519-custom-gpt-retirement-and-migration-faq)). If you haven't been told you have a deferral, plan on Dec. 11.

OpenAI's replacement is a plugin, or a project for ongoing work like writing, research or planning ([OpenAI custom GPT FAQ](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001519-custom-gpt-retirement-and-migration-faq)). Per the Sept. 23, 2026 release note, "Live now supports plugins on web, iOS, and Android" ([ChatGPT release notes](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/6825453-chatgpt-release-notes)). That means the move is: migrate the GPT, then test the replacement in Live voice. Don't test the old GPT in Live and assume. OpenAI also warns that custom actions "do not transfer automatically" ([OpenAI custom GPT FAQ](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001519-custom-gpt-retirement-and-migration-faq)), so leave extra time if your GPT looks things up in another system.

This is the second OpenAI retirement date we've flagged this month. Our [Nov. 12 post on the GPT-4o API shutdown](/blog/gpt-4o-api-shutdown-jan-14-2027) covers the Jan. 14, 2027 one for anything built on the API. The lesson carries over: find what depends on the old thing, then test the new thing on real work.

## The one-afternoon test

You need one real task, two runs and a punch list.

**Pick the task you'd miss most.** Not a demo question. The thing you actually say to ChatGPT on a Tuesday.

**Write down what "right" looks like first.** Three to five details that must come back correctly: names, amounts, addresses, the next step. Do this before you hear any answer, so you're grading against your list and not your mood.

**Run it on your current voice while you still can.** That window closes Dec. 11. Say the same thing both times. Reading from a card helps.

**Run it on Live.** Same words, same setting if you can manage it. If the real thing happens in the truck, test in the truck.

**Keep the punch list.** Four lines, each marked better, same or worse:

- **Accuracy of details:** did every item on your "right" list come back correct?
- **Length:** did it answer in the length you'd use, or did you have to cut it off?
- **Follow-up continuity:** ask a follow-up that depends on something you said two turns ago. Did it hold on?
- **Tone:** if it's a rehearsal or a customer draft, would you send it or say it as is?

**Fix what you can, then decide.** If answers run long, say how long you want them and run it again. If a detail keeps slipping, that task may belong in text, where you can read it before it goes anywhere.

## A worked example

**This is an example task, not a statistic or a test we ran.** Say a two-truck plumbing company's owner does a voice recap at the end of each day. He says something like: "Three jobs today. Martinez on Elm, water heater, quoted $1,850, she wants to think about it. Office park on Route 9, replaced two shutoff valves, invoice tomorrow. Johnson, slow drain, no charge, follow up in two weeks." Then he asks for a recap and a short follow-up text to Martinez.

His "right" list before he starts: the three customer names, the $1,850 quote, the two-week follow-up, and a text to Martinez that doesn't sound pushy.

His punch list might look like this:

- **Details:** Live got all three jobs and the quote. Worse on one point: it dropped "invoice tomorrow."
- **Length:** better. The recap fit in a sentence or two per job.
- **Continuity:** he asked, "Change the Martinez text to mention we can do Saturday." It kept the quote and the name. Same as before.
- **Tone:** the text read friendlier than he'd write it. He asked for plainer and got it.

Result: keep the workflow on Live, and add one line to the routine, "repeat back every next step." Not perfect, but every miss is one he caught and can correct.

If he also had a custom GPT that held his price sheet and his staff talked to it by voice, that GPT is the second item on his list this week: migrate it, then run the same test on the replacement in Live.

## What to watch after Dec. 11

Two quick checks. If you rely on live video or screen-sharing in voice, that stays on Advanced voice for now, and OpenAI hasn't put a date on when GPT-Live picks it up ([OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001638-retiring-advanced-and-standard-voice-in-chatgpt)). And if your team is on a Business workspace, open Settings > Voice and confirm Live is there for them. When GPT-Live-1 launched, OpenAI said it was "not available in ChatGPT Business, Enterprise, or Edu workspaces at launch" ([ChatGPT release notes](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/6825453-chatgpt-release-notes)), and the retirement article doesn't address workspaces.
