If your staff use the Copilot Chat that comes with their Microsoft 365 seats, some of them may now hit a wall partway through the workday. Microsoft started adding fair-use limits to more Copilot Chat features on Nov. 2, 2026, for organizations with 2,000 or fewer Microsoft 365 seats, and on Nov. 9 for larger ones ([Microsoft Partner Center](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/announcements/2026-november)).

A small business is in the first group. Take a hypothetical office with 12 people on Business Standard and nobody on a Copilot license: that team has been under the new rules for more than two weeks.

Copilot Chat itself isn't going away. Microsoft says it "remains included in Microsoft 365 subscriptions, and no admin action is required" ([Microsoft Partner Center](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/announcements/2026-november)). What changed is how much of it an unlicensed user gets, and when.

## What Microsoft actually said

We read the Partner Center announcement, the two Message Center notices and the Microsoft Support article. Here's what they agree on.

- **Who's affected.** Users without a Microsoft 365 Copilot license in commercial tenants, using Copilot Chat in apps on web, desktop and mobile ([MC1536158](https://mc.merill.net/message/MC1536158)).
- **What's new.** Some features, such as image generation, already had limits. Microsoft is "applying this approach to additional Copilot Chat features and capabilities," and MC1536158 says the new limits are "currently rolling out" ([MC1536158](https://mc.merill.net/message/MC1536158)). The companion notice for larger tenants says those limits "will start rolling out on November 9" ([MC1536163](https://mc.merill.net/message/MC1536163)). The Nov. 2 start for organizations with 2,000 or fewer seats is from Partner Center, not from those notices ([Microsoft Partner Center](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/announcements/2026-november)).
- **How much access you get can change during the day.** Microsoft says fair use "can vary throughout the day" ([MC1536158](https://mc.merill.net/message/MC1536158)).
- **Nobody can see the number.** Separately, "Microsoft sets and manages these limits. Admins can't view or adjust the limits" ([MC1536158](https://mc.merill.net/message/MC1536158)). The Support article adds that "Microsoft does not publish or share specific numeric limits" ([Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365-copilot/standard-versus-priority-access-to-features-in-microsoft-365-copilot-chat)).
- **Work in progress finishes.** "Responses already in progress will not be interrupted" ([MC1536158](https://mc.merill.net/message/MC1536158)).
- **Users get told.** The user "can try the feature or capability again later," and Microsoft says it "will be clear on the timeframe in product" ([MC1536158](https://mc.merill.net/message/MC1536158)).

None of these sources list which features beyond image generation are now limited. So we can't tell you whether drafting a long email or summarizing a file is the thing that trips it. Nobody outside Microsoft can, and the Support article says availability "can vary based on service conditions" ([Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365-copilot/standard-versus-priority-access-to-features-in-microsoft-365-copilot-chat)).

The line we'd hand to whoever answers "Copilot is broken" tickets:

> You may be unable to use a feature because you've reached a limit. This isn't an error or product malfunction.

That's from [Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365-copilot/standard-versus-priority-access-to-features-in-microsoft-365-copilot-chat). Reaching a limit "is expected behavior," the Partner Center note says ([Microsoft Partner Center](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/announcements/2026-november)).

## The notice comes with a buy button

Read the Partner Center version closely, because it's written for the people who sell you licenses. Its lead sentence says the limits "create an upsell conversation with your customers" ([Microsoft Partner Center](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/announcements/2026-november)).

Microsoft also says that in most organizations, the limit notification "includes an option to upgrade." Users can select it to request a Microsoft 365 Copilot license from their admin, who approves or denies it in the Microsoft 365 admin center. If your organization lets users buy licenses directly, they "might also see an option to purchase a license" ([Microsoft Partner Center](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/announcements/2026-november)).

And your reseller may already know who's hitting the wall. Microsoft says a partner tool called ASPX has a "Users Experiencing Usage Limits" view that "shows which of your customers have users reaching Copilot usage limits" ([Microsoft Partner Center](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/announcements/2026-november)). Expect a call.

None of that is sinister. It does mean the first person to suggest Copilot Business after a limit message may be the one selling it. Microsoft frames a user reaching a limit as something that "might indicate interest" in a license ([Microsoft Partner Center](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/announcements/2026-november)). We'd frame it as one person getting stuck once.

## Treat it as a workflow risk first

Here's how we'd sort a limit message when it shows up.

**Someone hit it once, on something that could wait.** Do nothing. The notice tells them when the feature comes back, and their half-finished response wasn't cut off ([Microsoft Partner Center](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/announcements/2026-november)).

**Someone hits it repeatedly, on a job with a deadline.** That's the case worth paying for. Two hypothetical examples: the office manager who uses Copilot Chat to pull together the weekly vendor summary every Friday, or the estimator who drafts every client proposal in it. If that work stalls, that's a real cost.

**Lots of people hit it, on nice-to-have tasks.** That's a usage pattern, not a licensing gap. Licensing all of them buys capacity for things nobody would miss.

The rule: license the seat that's blocked on work that matters, not the whole team because one person saw a notice.

## What one seat costs versus the team

Microsoft's business pricing page lists the Microsoft 365 Copilot Business add-on as "Originally starting from $21.00 now starting from $18.00," or $18.00 per user per month, paid yearly ([Microsoft Copilot pricing](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/copilot/pricing/business)). Microsoft's fine print says the discount runs from July 1 through Dec. 31, 2026, requires an annual commitment, applies to the first year only, and requires an existing eligible Microsoft 365 Business plan ([Microsoft](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/copilot/business)).

Our math uses a hypothetical 10-person office already on Microsoft 365 Business seats, at the prices on [Microsoft's pricing page](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/copilot/pricing/business).

**Upgrade 1 seat:**

- Promo, first year: 1 × $18 × 12 = **$216**
- List, after the promo year: 1 × $21 × 12 = **$252** a year

**Upgrade all 10 seats:**

- Promo, first year: 10 × $18 × 12 = **$2,160**
- List, after the promo year: 10 × $21 × 12 = **$2,520** a year

Licensing the whole office costs $1,944 more in year one than licensing the one person who's blocked ($2,160 minus $216). At list price, the gap is $2,268 a year ($2,520 minus $252).

Two more things from the same page. The license fee isn't the whole bill: Microsoft says "some AI experiences require usage-based billing and may result in charges based on usage, beyond your license fees" ([Microsoft Copilot pricing](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/copilot/pricing/business)). Our [Copilot spending meter guide](/blog/microsoft-copilot-spending-meter-cap) covers how to cap that. And after a paid subscription starts, Microsoft gives a 7-day cancellation window for a prorated refund ([Microsoft Copilot pricing](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/copilot/pricing/business)). That's your window to confirm the one seat solved the problem.

## What to do this week

Microsoft recommends two things for admins: tell the help desk, and review license request settings ([MC1536158](https://mc.merill.net/message/MC1536158)). Here's what that looks like in practice.

**Brief whoever handles IT.** Give them the blockquote above. Tell them a limit notice isn't an outage, and ask them to log who saw it, on what task, and how often.

**Decide whether staff can buy licenses themselves.** In the Microsoft 365 admin center, go to Settings > Org settings, open the Services tab and select Self-service trials and purchases. Select a product to set it to Allow, Allow for trials only, or Do not allow ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/commerce/subscriptions/manage-self-service-purchases-admins)). Microsoft says new products default to allowing self-service purchase, and the setting works product by product ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/commerce/subscriptions/manage-self-service-purchases-admins)).

**Check the request queue.** Go to Billing > Licenses and open the Requests tab. Each request shows the product, the requester, the date and the status, and requests are held for 12 months ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/commerce/licenses/manage-license-requests)). You need at least the User Administrator or License Administrator role ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/commerce/licenses/manage-license-requests)).

**Send requests to your own process, if you have one.** On the same Requests tab, select Connect your request process, check Use my organization's request process, and type the message staff should see ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/commerce/licenses/manage-license-requests)). Something like "Email the office manager with the task you were doing" turns a buy button into a conversation.

**Approve one at a time.** When a request lands, you can approve some users and reject others in the same request ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/commerce/licenses/manage-license-requests)). Approve the person who's blocked on real work.
