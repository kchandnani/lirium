If your team was going to move from a pile of personal ChatGPT Plus accounts to a real Business workspace sometime this winter, Monday is the day that matters. OpenAI says a Business workspace created "from November 23 through November 30, 2026" gets "double the weekly included usage on eligible Standard and Premium seats through December 31, 2026" ([OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8980713-sign-up-for-chatgpt-business)). Today is Thursday, Nov. 26. Nov. 30 is a Monday, so you have four days.

It isn't a discount. Seats cost the listed prices. You get a bigger allowance for about a month, so use it as a test, not a reason to buy.

## What OpenAI actually says

The Help Center adds the line that matters for a growing team:

> The offer also applies to eligible seats you add to that new workspace through December 31.

That's from the [OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8980713-sign-up-for-chatgpt-business). So the date that counts is when the workspace is created, not when each person joins. Example: create it by Nov. 30, add the rest of the team on Dec. 10, and per OpenAI those eligible seats get the doubled usage too.

The pricing page runs a shorter banner: "Sign up for ChatGPT Business by Nov 30 and get 2x included usage and fast mode at no extra cost for all seats through Dec 31" ([OpenAI pricing](https://openai.com/business/pricing/)). The banner adds fast mode, which the Help article doesn't mention, and says "all seats" where the Help article says "eligible" seats in new workspaces. When the banner and the support article disagree, we plan on the narrower one. Treat fast mode as a pricing page promise and check it once you're in.

Neither page says what time zone "November 30" means. Sign up before the weekend ends.

## If you already have a Business workspace, sit this out

A new-workspaces-only offer invites the obvious workaround. OpenAI shuts that door in the same article: "If you want to renew or reactivate an existing workspace, use that workspace's billing or reactivation options; creating another workspace does not restore its subscription or data" ([OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8980713-sign-up-for-chatgpt-business)). If your company already has one, OpenAI says to "ask a workspace owner for an invitation."

A second workspace would likely mean a second bill and a team split across two places, all for a month of headroom. If your current workspace runs out of usage, OpenAI lists the real options: "wait for it to reset, upgrade your plan, or buy credits" ([OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/articles/20001625)).

## What happens to everyone's Plus account

OpenAI treats joining Business and moving your stuff into it as two separate steps.

**Joining doesn't move or cancel anything.** "Joining Business does not automatically migrate your content or cancel your personal subscription" ([OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8980713-sign-up-for-chatgpt-business)). If someone keeps their personal workspace separate, "any personal subscription continues until you cancel it," and canceling it "does not delete your personal chats" ([OpenAI migration guide](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8801890-migrating-your-personal-workspace-to-chatgpt-business-or-enterprise)). So until each person does something, you're paying twice for anyone who keeps Plus: their Plus and your Business seat.

**Merging does cancel it.** If a person chooses to merge their personal workspace into Business, "an active personal subscription is automatically canceled and the unused portion is refunded, except for subscriptions purchased through a mobile app store" ([OpenAI migration guide](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8801890-migrating-your-personal-workspace-to-chatgpt-business-or-enterprise)). Anyone who subscribed through the Apple App Store or Google Play must cancel there.

**Merging is one way.** OpenAI says migration has no undo that moves content from Business back to your personal workspace, "self-service data export is not available" in Business, and if someone leaves the company workspace they "may also lose access to that content." The personal workspace is deactivated, not deleted ([OpenAI migration guide](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8801890-migrating-your-personal-workspace-to-chatgpt-business-or-enterprise)). Download any export you need from the personal workspace before merging.

## Use December as a stress test, with one caveat

OpenAI lists two seat types: Standard is "Best for everyday work." Premium is "More usage than standard, with no 5-hour limit" ([OpenAI pricing](https://openai.com/business/pricing/)). None of the pages we read says how much usage either one includes. That's the question December can help answer, cheaply.

Here's how we'd run it: put your heaviest user on a Premium seat and everyone else on Standard. Every Friday, have the Premium user and one typical Standard user check Settings > Usage, where OpenAI says you'll be able to see "your combined remaining usage and reset times" ([OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/articles/20001625)). By month's end you'll know whether your heavy user needs Premium or just wants it.

The caveat: everything you measure in December runs at double, so a Standard seat that never hits its limit in December could hit it in January. Halve what you see. If your typical user burned through more than half of a doubled week, they'll likely run short at the regular level.

December brings a second change that muddies the reading. The pricing page says "Starting in December, plan usage will have a shared limit across all of ChatGPT and Codex" ([OpenAI pricing](https://openai.com/business/pricing/)). The linked Help article says usage "will be measured in tokens," that more advanced models and complex tasks "generally use more of your allowance," and that "Instant chat will not draw from your usage" ([OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/articles/20001625)). OpenAI says "We expect most users won't need to wait for their usage to reset more often than they do now," a forecast, not a measurement. Codex work and chats draw from one allowance, so test with real work.

## A worked example: 5 Standard seats

**This is an example team, not a statistic.** Say a five-person accounting office moves everyone to Standard seats. OpenAI's prices: Standard is "$20/month if billed annually. $25/month if billed monthly." Premium is "$100/month if billed annually. $125/month if billed monthly" ([OpenAI pricing](https://openai.com/business/pricing/)). The Help article matches.

- Annual: 5 × $20 = $100 a month, or $100 × 12 = $1,200 a year
- Monthly: 5 × $25 = $125 a month, or $125 × 12 = $1,500 a year

The gap is $125 minus $100 = $25 a month, or $300 a year. Annual billing costs 20% less than monthly ($25 ÷ $125).

Now say the December test shows one person really does need Premium. Totals for all five seats: on monthly billing that's 4 × $25 + $125 = $225 a month, up $100 from the all-Standard $125. On annual billing it's 4 × $20 + $100 = $180 a month, up $80 from the all-Standard $100.

Our read: if you're signing up this weekend to run the test, monthly is the honest choice. The extra $25 a month for the five seats is the price of staying flexible, though we found no OpenAI page that spells out cancellation terms, so check at checkout. Go annual once you know your seat mix. The Help article says you pick monthly or annual at checkout and that "new capacity and upgrades are charged immediately on a prorated basis" ([OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8980713-sign-up-for-chatgpt-business)).

One wrinkle on team size. The Help article says you can start with "1 paid Standard or Premium seat" and that seats count toward a "200-seat limit" ([OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8980713-sign-up-for-chatgpt-business)). The pricing page describes Business as for teams of 2 to 200 employees, and its FAQ says Business plans are "available starting at 2 users" ([OpenAI pricing](https://openai.com/business/pricing/)). For five people it doesn't matter.

## The extras on the pricing page

The pricing page also lists "Up to $500 per month in API credits for your workspace," with a footnote that "Activation and eligibility requirements apply," the first grant is prorated and unused credits expire ([OpenAI pricing](https://openai.com/business/pricing/)). Don't budget for "up to" until it shows in your account. Dots, OpenAI's always-on agents, are in the included-features list, and the comparison table marks them "Business Premium" without saying more.
