If you pay Zendesk per agent seat, there's now a second way to pay. On Nov. 10, 2026, Zendesk announced Zendesk Unlimited, a set of Outcome AI Platform plans with unlimited seats, AI features that vary by tier, and an Outcome Budget that pays for "eligible issues resolved and business work completed" ([Zendesk newsroom](https://www.zendesk.com/newsroom/articles/zendesk-upends-enterprise-service-with-outcomeai-platform-unlimited-seats/)).

You can't buy it off a pricing page. The plans are in an Early Access Program, and Zendesk tells customers to contact Sales about eligibility, included capabilities and pricing ([Zendesk Help Center](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/11443724675098-Announcing-Outcome-Based-Pricing-by-Zendesk-EAP)).

So the number that decides whether this saves you money, the price of one outcome, isn't public. That's the first thing to get from Sales. The second is the exact definition of what counts as an outcome. With both in hand, you can price your last 90 days of tickets and know the answer before you sign.

## What Zendesk actually said

We read the newsroom release, the Help Center announcement and Zendesk's product blog post. Here's what they agree on.

- **No per-seat charges.** Customers "give people access without per-seat charges" ([Zendesk Help Center](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/11443724675098-Announcing-Outcome-Based-Pricing-by-Zendesk-EAP)).
- **Each plan includes an outcome budget.** It's set "in the customer's contracted currency" and spent on "eligible conversational and operational outcomes." Plan fees, included capabilities and budget terms "vary by plan" ([Zendesk Help Center](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/11443724675098-Announcing-Outcome-Based-Pricing-by-Zendesk-EAP)).
- **Two kinds of outcome.** Zendesk says a Conversational Outcome is "when an issue is successfully addressed within a customer interaction," and an Operational AI Outcome is "when an automated workflow is completed across business systems" ([Zendesk blog](https://www.zendesk.com/blog/product-news/introducing-zendesk-outcome-ai/)).
- **Zendesk grades the work.** It says it "evaluates each outcome for quality and completeness, so Zendesk customers can see what counts toward their budget," and that customers "can start with a low upfront investment" ([Zendesk newsroom](https://www.zendesk.com/newsroom/articles/zendesk-upends-enterprise-service-with-outcomeai-platform-unlimited-seats/)).
- **You can trace the bill.** The Help Center says customers can review which capabilities draw from the budget and "trace usage to individual outcomes" ([Zendesk Help Center](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/11443724675098-Announcing-Outcome-Based-Pricing-by-Zendesk-EAP)).

The line we'd underline before any sales call:

> Not every interaction or use of an AI capability is a billable outcome.

That's from the [Zendesk Help Center](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/11443724675098-Announcing-Outcome-Based-Pricing-by-Zendesk-EAP). It's reassuring, and it's also the whole question. Which interactions are billable is what you need on paper.

## Treat 84% as a pitch, not a plan

The newsroom release says that with the platform, Zendesk customers "can automate up to 84% of service requests" ([Zendesk newsroom](https://www.zendesk.com/newsroom/articles/zendesk-upends-enterprise-service-with-outcomeai-platform-unlimited-seats/)). That's Zendesk describing its own product. "Up to" means a ceiling, and the release doesn't say whose tickets, what kind, or how it was measured. We cover how to read claims like that in [our FTC vendor claims guide](/blog/ftc-ai-vendor-claims-red-flags).

There's a second reason not to plan around a high number. Under outcome pricing, more automation means a bigger outcome bill. A seat costs the same whether the AI closes a handful of your tickets or most of them. An outcome budget doesn't.

## Get two things in writing

**The rate card.** Ask for the price of one Conversational Outcome and one Operational AI Outcome, the plan fee, and what the plan includes. Zendesk says those terms vary by plan ([Zendesk Help Center](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/11443724675098-Announcing-Outcome-Based-Pricing-by-Zendesk-EAP)), so get the one for the plan you'd actually buy. None of the three sources say what happens when the budget runs out, or whether unused budget carries over. Ask both.

**The billable definition.** Compare it, line by line, with a definition that is public. Intercom's FAQ says a Fin outcome counts when "a customer confirms their issue is resolved," when "they don't ask for more help after Fin responds," or when Fin "completes a workflow (Procedure), including handoffs." It also says you pay nothing if "the customer asks to speak to a human," that you're "only charged at most once per conversation," and that "if the conversation is later reopened, the outcome is refunded" ([Intercom pricing](https://www.intercom.com/pricing)).

Those are the clauses to ask Zendesk about directly. Does silence count as resolved? Is a handoff to your team billable? Can one conversation produce more than one outcome? What happens on a reopen? And since Zendesk does the quality grading itself ([Zendesk newsroom](https://www.zendesk.com/newsroom/articles/zendesk-upends-enterprise-service-with-outcomeai-platform-unlimited-seats/)), can you dispute a charge?

## Price your last 90 days

Export the last 90 days of tickets and sort each one into three piles: closed in a single exchange with no human needed, closed by a workflow across systems (a refund, an address change), and needed a person. The first two piles are your candidate outcomes. Once you have Zendesk's definition, move tickets between piles to match it.

Here's our worked example. **Every input below is hypothetical,** chosen to show the math.

- A three-agent team handled **1,800 tickets** in 90 days, or 600 a month.
- After sorting, **900** of them (50%) fit the outcome definition.
- The current help desk invoice for the quarter is **$1,500**. That's a made-up figure, not a Zendesk price. Use your own invoice.

**Zendesk.** The cost is: plan fee + (900 × the per-outcome rate from Sales). We can't fill in either number, because Zendesk hasn't published them ([Zendesk Help Center](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/11443724675098-Announcing-Outcome-Based-Pricing-by-Zendesk-EAP)). What we can do is find the ceiling. To beat the $1,500 invoice, the rate has to be under ($1,500 minus the plan fee) ÷ 900. With no plan fee at all, that's $1.67 per outcome. With a $600 quarterly plan fee (again hypothetical), it's $900 ÷ 900, or $1.00.

**Intercom, for comparison.** Fin is $0.99 per outcome on every plan ([Intercom pricing](https://www.intercom.com/pricing)). The same 900 outcomes cost 900 × $0.99 = **$891** for the quarter, before seats. Intercom's page now lists its Essential seat at $19 per seat per month for new customers, paid annually, down from $29 ([Intercom pricing](https://www.intercom.com/pricing)). Three seats for three months is 3 × $19 × 3 = **$171**, for a total of **$1,062**.

**At Zendesk's 84%.** If you plugged the vendor's ceiling into the same 1,800 tickets, you'd get 1,512 outcomes ([Zendesk newsroom](https://www.zendesk.com/newsroom/articles/zendesk-upends-enterprise-service-with-outcomeai-platform-unlimited-seats/)). At Intercom's rate ([Intercom pricing](https://www.intercom.com/pricing)) that's 1,512 × $0.99 = **$1,496.88** in outcome charges alone, almost the whole hypothetical invoice. Even a modest-sounding rate gets expensive when the AI is busy.

Our earlier [Fin break-even post](/blog/intercom-fin-break-even-math) works the other side of this: whether an outcome costs less than the human minutes it replaces. Run both. This post asks whether outcome billing beats your seat bill. That one asks whether the AI beats your team.

## What to do this week

**Pull the export.** Ninety days of tickets, sorted into the three piles above. You'll need it no matter what Sales says.

**Book the call with a list.** Rate per Conversational Outcome, rate per Operational AI Outcome, plan fee, budget overage terms, rollover, and the written definition of billable. Zendesk directs those questions to Sales ([Zendesk Help Center](https://support.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/11443724675098-Announcing-Outcome-Based-Pricing-by-Zendesk-EAP)).

**Run the ceiling math.** Put your invoice and their plan fee into the formula. If the quoted rate is above the ceiling, your seats are cheaper at your volume.

**Recheck at a higher automation rate.** If the AI improves, your bill grows. Rerun the numbers at double your current outcome count and see if you still like them.
