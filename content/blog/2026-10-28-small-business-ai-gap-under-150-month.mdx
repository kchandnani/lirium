The smallest businesses are the ones sitting out AI, and money isn't the reason it should be. In Homebase's 2026 Main Street AI Gap Report, 54% of businesses under $500K in annual revenue are using AI, compared with 81% of businesses at $5M or more. Yet among adopters, the most common monthly AI spend is $41 to $150, and 13% pay nothing extra because they use AI features already built into their tools ([Homebase](https://www.joinhomebase.com/blog/small-business-ai-gap-report)).

Our take: a shop under $500K can get a real start for about $118 a month with three tools, before payroll. Below is the stack, priced from each vendor this week, and where $150 stops being enough.

## Read the survey for what it is

Homebase sells scheduling and payroll software, so this is a vendor-run survey with a stake in the answer. Propeller Insights fielded it with 750 U.S. owners, executives, directors and managers in payroll, scheduling, timekeeping or HR, at businesses with 5 to 499 employees and hourly teams ([Homebase](https://www.joinhomebase.com/blog/small-business-ai-gap-report)).

Three caveats:

- **No solo shops.** The floor is five employees. Many businesses under $500K have fewer, so they aren't in this sample.
- **The year-over-year jump compares different groups.** Overall use rose to 74% "using or piloting AI tools," up from 64% in 2025. The 2025 survey covered 828 decision-makers at businesses with fewer than 200 employees ([Homebase](https://www.joinhomebase.com/blog/small-business-ai-gap-report)). Homebase calls the change statistically significant. It doesn't publish a margin of error or 2026 field dates.
- **The spend band is for adopters only.** "$41 to $150" describes what people already using AI pay, not what a holdout would need.

The revenue gap is the finding we'd trust most: a 27-point spread is too wide to be a sampling quirk.

## What the "$343 a month" covers

Homebase says owners using AI for both scheduling and payroll save "an estimated 7.7 hours and $343 a month (about $4,100 a year)," and that's "on those two tasks alone" ([Homebase](https://www.joinhomebase.com/blog/small-business-ai-gap-report)).

Two limits. First, it's both tasks together; scheduling-only savings aren't broken out. Second, these are respondents' estimates, and the report doesn't say how the dollar figure was built. Divide $343 by 7.7 hours and you get about $44.55 an hour; that's our arithmetic, not Homebase's method.

Treat $343 as a ceiling to test against, not a number to budget on. Time the work for two weeks before you buy anything.

## The starter stack: $118 a month

Built for one location, an owner plus one office manager, and a handful of hourly staff. Homebase Plus and Claude Pro are month-to-month. Google's $14 rate below is its annual-commitment list price billed monthly; Flexible (cancel anytime) costs more.

**1. Homebase Plus, $70 a month.** One location, unlimited employees, and the tier that lists Homebase's "AI-powered Scheduling Assistant." Paid annually it's $56 ([Homebase pricing](https://www.joinhomebase.com/pricing)). What it replaces: the Sunday-night spreadsheet and the group text where shifts get traded. Honest flag: free Basic already covers scheduling and time tracking for up to 10 employees at one location ([Homebase pricing](https://www.joinhomebase.com/pricing)). If your schedule barely changes, start on Basic and spend the $70 elsewhere.

**2. Claude Pro, $20 a month, for the owner.** Monthly price; annually it's $17 a month ($200 up front) ([Claude pricing](https://claude.com/pricing)). ChatGPT Plus is also $20 a month and does the same job here ([ChatGPT pricing](https://chatgpt.com/pricing)). Pick one, not both. What it replaces: the blank page—quotes, job posts, a reply to an angry review, a first read of a P&L export, a handbook draft you then take to someone qualified.

**3. Google Workspace Business Standard, $14 per user a month on the annual plan, two users: $28.** That $14 is Google's annual/fixed-term list price. Flexible (cancel anytime) is $16.80 per user, or $33.60 for two ([Google Workspace Help](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/billing/compare-flexible-and-annual-fixed-term-payment-plans)). Standard includes Gemini in Gmail, Docs, Meet and more, appointment booking, and eSignature. Google's pricing page shows a 50% intro discount for new customers on the annual rates; we used the full $14 ([Google Workspace pricing](https://workspace.google.com/pricing)). What it replaces: separate tools for business email, booking and signing, plus the notes nobody took on the supplier call. If you already pay for email, part of this $28 is a swap.

**Running total on the annual Workspace rate:** $70 + $20 + $28 = **$118 a month**, leaving $32 under $150. On Flexible Workspace: $70 + $20 + $33.60 = **$123.60**.

Cheaper path: Workspace Business Starter at $7 per user on the annual plan, or $8.40 Flexible ([Google Workspace Help](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/billing/compare-flexible-and-annual-fixed-term-payment-plans)). You keep Gemini in Gmail but lose it in Docs, Sheets and Meet. That's $70 + $20 + $14 = **$104** on the annual rate.

## A real example of the chat seat earning its keep

OpenAI published the story of Morgan Sterling, who runs Beach Cities Builder, a general contractor in Hermosa Beach. It's OpenAI's own customer story, so read it as a best case. Sterling gets several subcontractor invoices a day across roughly 30 active job folders. He used ChatGPT to build a script that collects each invoice, reads it, identifies the job and files it. OpenAI says the first version took about two hours to build and now saves him "roughly 5-6 hours a week" ([OpenAI Academy](https://academy.openai.com/en/public/blogs/how-chatgpt-became-a-contractors-back-office-2026-05-07)).

Take the pattern, not the hours: automate one repetitive job first.

## Does $118 pay for itself?

Illustrative math, not data. At $25 an hour for an office manager, break-even is $118 ÷ $25 = 4.72 hours a month, a bit over an hour a week. At the $44.55 an hour implied by Homebase's figures, it's $118 ÷ $44.55 = 2.65 hours a month.

An hour a week is a low bar for a schedule that used to eat Sunday night. If you can't find that hour after a month, cancel. Homebase and Claude are month to month; Workspace on Flexible is too.

> At this budget, AI should take one chore off your list. If you can't name the chore, you're not ready to buy the stack.

## What to skip, and when $150 isn't enough

**Skip the second chatbot.** Claude Pro plus ChatGPT Plus is $40 a month for mostly overlapping work.

**Skip annual-only add-ons for now.** Microsoft 365 Copilot Business is $18 per user a month, down from $21, but paid yearly and on top of an eligible Microsoft 365 plan ([Microsoft](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/copilot/pricing/business)). Fine if you already live in Outlook and Excel. It doesn't fit a month-to-month trial.

**Payroll is where the $150 ceiling breaks.** Homebase's quoted savings cover scheduling and payroll together. The payroll add-on is free for six months under a current offer, then $49 a month plus $6 per employee paid ([Homebase pricing](https://www.joinhomebase.com/pricing)). With six employees that's $49 + $36 = $85, and the stack goes to $203—past our $150 starter budget once payroll is in. Homebase also advertises $29 a month for teams up to five, with terms that apply—read them before you count on that price. If you already pay for payroll elsewhere, compare that bill, not $150.

**Two chat seats.** If the office manager needs Claude too, Claude Team is $25 per seat billed monthly, for teams of 2 to 150 ([Claude pricing](https://claude.com/pricing)). Two seats run $50, replacing the $20 Pro seat, which takes the stack to $148. Right at the edge. Team seats also pool API credit for a later agent build; see [our Claude Team API credit post](/blog/claude-team-500-api-credit-first-agent).

## What we'd do this week

1. Time your scheduling, invoicing and email for two weeks.
2. Pick the one chore that eats the most hours.
3. Buy only the tool that hits it, month to month where you can.
4. After 30 days, compare hours saved against what you paid.
