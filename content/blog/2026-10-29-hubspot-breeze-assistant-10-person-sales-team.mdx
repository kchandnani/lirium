Breeze Assistant itself is free. The three parts of HubSpot's fall release that matter to a sales team are not. To get self-updating deal records, the Prospecting Agent and AI-drafted quotes for 10 reps, you need two paid hubs, Sales Hub Professional and Revenue Hub Professional. On annual billing with HubSpot's current new-customer discount on Revenue Hub, that's $1,470 a month in seats plus a required $1,500 onboarding fee, before you spend a single extra credit ([HubSpot Sales pricing](https://www.hubspot.com/pricing/sales), [HubSpot Revenue pricing](https://www.hubspot.com/pricing/revenue)). Two of the three pieces are also still in beta.

Our take: worth a pilot for a team that already quotes inside HubSpot. Not worth buying on the headline numbers.

## What the 3x figures do and don't show

HubSpot's September 16 Spotlight post says that when AI runs on good HubSpot data, "businesses can create 3.6x more MQLs, win 3.2x more deals, and close over 2x more tickets" ([HubSpot](https://www.hubspot.com/company-news/fall-26-spotlight)). The footnote reads, in full:

"Data based on outcomes for Professional and Enterprise HubSpot customers using AI with high quality context versus Professional and Enterprise customers not using AI" ([HubSpot](https://www.hubspot.com/company-news/fall-26-spotlight)).

That's a comparison of two groups of customers, not a test. Companies that adopt AI early and keep clean CRM data are probably better run to begin with. The footnote gives no sample size, time period, or definition of "high quality context." It tells you HubSpot's AI users win more deals. It doesn't tell you the AI is the reason.

## What each piece requires

**Breeze Assistant** comes "with every HubSpot subscription at no additional cost" ([HubSpot](https://www.hubspot.com/products/artificial-intelligence/breeze-ai-assistant)). The work it hands off to agents is where the paid tiers come in.

**The self-updating CRM.** For a sales team, this is deal progression, which reads calls, meetings and emails and suggests or auto-applies deal property updates. It requires Sales Hub Professional or Enterprise and an assigned Sales Seat, and a Super Admin has to enroll in the "Deal Progression: Next Best Actions and Deal Plans public beta" ([HubSpot Knowledge Base](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/records/move-deals-forward-with-deal-progression)). Updates to default properties are free. Updates to custom or methodology properties run through the data agent and use credits ([same page](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/records/move-deals-forward-with-deal-progression)).

**The Prospecting Agent** runs on any Sales Hub Starter, Professional or Enterprise plan and uses credits ([HubSpot Knowledge Base](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/prospecting/use-the-prospecting-agent)). The signal watching, though, sits in a beta called "Prospecting Agent Gets Smarter: Buying Signals and Contact Sourcing" ([HubSpot Knowledge Base](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/sales-tools/use-ai-to-close-deals-faster)). The "40+ buying signals" figure appears only in the Spotlight post ([HubSpot](https://www.hubspot.com/company-news/fall-26-spotlight)). We couldn't find the list on a product or help page.

**AI quotes.** Revenue Hub is the real, current name. HubSpot calls it "the evolution of Commerce Hub" ([HubSpot](https://www.hubspot.com/products/revenue)). Quotes need Revenue Hub Professional or Enterprise, and each person creating quotes needs a Revenue Hub seat ([HubSpot Knowledge Base](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/quotes/create-and-send-quotes)). Sales Hub no longer gets you quotes: legacy quotes "are no longer available to new users" ([HubSpot Knowledge Base](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/quotes/compare-legacy-quotes-and-quotes)).

## One deal, lead to signed quote

Here's how those pieces line up for a 10-person team selling, say, software subscriptions to mid-sized companies.

**Lead.** A rep builds a play: target segment, the signals that matter (HubSpot's examples include funding rounds, leadership changes and site visits), and personas ([HubSpot](https://www.hubspot.com/products/sales/ai-prospecting-agent)). The agent recommends accounts, finds contacts through providers such as ZoomInfo and Apollo, and drafts outreach ([HubSpot Knowledge Base](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/sales-tools/use-ai-to-close-deals-faster)). Our rule: the rep reads every draft before it goes out.

**Meeting.** The discovery call runs through HubSpot's Notetaker or a connected Zoom, Teams or Google Meet. Afterward, deal progression proposes a new stage, close date and next steps, and the rep accepts or rejects each one ([HubSpot Knowledge Base](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/records/move-deals-forward-with-deal-progression)). "Self-updating" is a setting: by default you get suggestions to review, and auto-updates are something you turn on.

**Quote.** The rep types something like "Create a quote for Example Deal using My Quote Template," and Breeze drafts an outline. The rep refines it, types "Proceed to create quote," then finishes in the quote editor ([HubSpot Knowledge Base](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/ai/generate-quotes-with-breeze-assistant)). The Spotlight says quotes "generate themselves" ([HubSpot](https://www.hubspot.com/company-news/fall-26-spotlight)). The help page describes a draft a person prompts, checks and sends.

**Signature.** Professional includes 25 e-signatures per user per month ([HubSpot](https://www.hubspot.com/pricing/revenue)).

The real example is Workleap, which the Spotlight calls a Canadian software company that got "from quote to signature in under 15 minutes" ([HubSpot](https://www.hubspot.com/company-news/fall-26-spotlight)). HubSpot's own case study is more specific. Workleap is a Montreal HR tech company with nearly 400 employees. It cut 12+ quote templates down to two, which the study says brought quote-to-signature time to "as little as 15 minutes." The example is one deal: a customer asked for a quantity change, the rep edited the quote, and the signature came back within 15 minutes ([HubSpot case study](https://www.hubspot.com/case-studies/workleap)). That's a good result from template cleanup and automatic proration. It isn't a typical cycle time, and the story doesn't credit AI-drafted quotes for it.

## The 10-seat bill

Billing terms we used: annual commitment, paid annually, for a new customer. Sales Hub Professional doesn't show a month-to-month option.

- **Sales Hub Professional:** $90 per seat a month paid annually, or $100 billed monthly on an annual commitment. Required one-time Professional Onboarding: $1,500 ([HubSpot](https://www.hubspot.com/pricing/sales)).
- **Revenue Hub Professional:** $85 paid annually, $95 billed monthly. New customers who buy Sales Hub pay $57 per seat, "billed annually" ([HubSpot](https://www.hubspot.com/pricing/revenue)). HubSpot doesn't say how long that price lasts. The Revenue Hub page lists no onboarding fee.

Every rep needs a Sales Seat for deal progression. Our assumption: all 10 reps also build their own quotes.

- Sales seats: 10 × $90 = $900
- Revenue seats: 10 × $57 = $570
- **Monthly: $1,470.** First year: $1,470 × 12 = $17,640, plus $1,500 onboarding = **$19,140**.

If only three people build quotes: $900 + (3 × $57) = $1,071 a month, or $12,852 + $1,500 = $14,352 in year one. Without the discount, at $85, the 10-and-10 setup costs $1,750 a month. Billed monthly, it's (10 × $100) + (10 × $95) = $1,950. HubSpot's seat pages don't say plainly whether one user can hold a Sales Seat and a Revenue Seat at once ([HubSpot Knowledge Base](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/account-management/manage-seats)). Get that in writing, along with what the $57 renews at.

## Where the credit meter runs

The account gets 3,000 HubSpot Credits a month. Included credits are "not additive across products," so buying both hubs still gets you 3,000, not 6,000. Unused credits don't roll over. Extra credits cost $0.010 each, or $10 per 1,000-credit monthly pack, and a pack stays on for the rest of your term ([HubSpot Products & Services Catalog](https://legal.hubspot.com/hubspot-product-and-services-catalog)).

The Prospecting Agent uses 100 credits to "recommend outreach for one lead," and HubSpot says play settings can add steps that use more ([HubSpot Knowledge Base](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/prospecting/use-the-prospecting-agent)). A data agent prompt on one record uses 10 ([same catalog](https://legal.hubspot.com/hubspot-product-and-services-catalog)). So 3,000 credits buys at most 30 agent-worked leads a month for the whole team. That's three per rep.

An illustration, not a forecast: each rep works 15 agent-sourced leads a month (150 × 100 = 15,000 credits), and the team approves 300 custom-property updates, which we assume bill as one 10-credit data agent prompt each (300 × 10 = 3,000). That's 18,000 credits, less 3,000 included, for 15,000 extra × $0.010 = $150 a month. That brings the 10-and-10 setup to $1,620 a month, or ($1,620 × 12) + $1,500 = $20,940 in year one. You can set credit limits for specific features ([HubSpot Products & Services Catalog](https://legal.hubspot.com/hubspot-product-and-services-catalog)), and users with billing permissions can pause the agent's credit use ([HubSpot Knowledge Base](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/prospecting/use-the-prospecting-agent)). Set those limits on day one.

> HubSpot's numbers show that teams using its AI win more deals. They don't show that the AI wins them. Your own pilot is the only test that counts.

## What we'd do this month

1. Use the 14-day free Prospecting Agent access, which doesn't touch your credits. You'll need a Professional or Enterprise plan, an assigned seat, and the buying-signals beta ([HubSpot Knowledge Base](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/prospecting/opt-in-to-14-days-of-free-access-to-the-prospecting-agent)).
2. Track three numbers for those two weeks: leads worked, replies, and drafts a rep had to rewrite.
3. Leave deal progression auto-updates off until reps stop rejecting suggestions. Our [post on who should check AI's work](/blog/small-business-ai-accuracy-worry) covers the review step.
4. Price your real credit burn before you sign the annual contract.
